Destacado · Marcos Marín

Beyond the Visible
The Symbol as Visual Energy

The exhibition presented by Ninoska Huerta Gallery offers an opportunity to enter a lesser known yet extraordinarily revealing territory within Marcos Marín’s artistic research.

In 2010, the artist turned his attention to the ancient symbols associated with Feng Shui, a millennia-old Chinese tradition rooted in the pursuit of harmony between the human being, space, and the forces that move through it.

Marín does not approach these symbols as ornamental elements or merely as cultural representations. He perceives them as forms charged with meaning and energy, subjecting them to a process of visual transformation. The symbol becomes structure; structure becomes rhythm; and rhythm becomes movement.

On paper, Marín reconstructs these signs through a rigorous geometric organization, creating compositions in which symmetry, repetition, and optical interaction generate a distinctive sense of vibration.

In this series, the eye does not merely observe: it participates.

Each work establishes a dialogue between what we recognize and what our perception can barely decipher. The forms seem to open, shift, and multiply, as if the energy contained within the symbol were finding a new dimension through the language of Op Art.

Thus, the ancient and the contemporary converge in a space where the symbol becomes a visual experience and geometry transforms into energy.

Ninoska Huerta

Amalia Pereira

Poetics of the Intimate Fragment

Amalia Pereira explores collage as a poetic language, integrating photography, drawing, and diverse elements into delicate small-format compositions. With a subtle surrealist sensibility, her work intertwines memory, nature, and the unconscious, creating intimate images that invite contemplation and reveal the beauty within the fragment.
